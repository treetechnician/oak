A plywood clubhouse with a working crank elevator

Four rooms, an open attic and a hand-cranked elevator, sized for the ~3″ licensed character figures it'll be themed with. Phase 1 is the woodworking. Phase 2 adds 3D-printed parts once the printer is running.

model: all checks pass every dimension generated from tools/house_model.py

How it's put together

A plywood box: two side walls, a middle floor in dadoes, an attic floor on top, two dividers with walk-through doorways, a pentagon facade screwed to the front, and a two-panel gable roof on pine eave cleats. The back is open for play. A 2″ porch on the base behind the rooms holds figures and helps stability.

Theme

The character theme comes from store-bought licensed pieces: a ~3″ Mickey figure set plus licensed wall decals or peel-and-stick wallpaper inside the rooms. Everything else here is original, so the paint colours are entirely up to you (and the kid). The rooms are sized to the figures. Buy the figure set first and measure a figure: if they're taller than 3¼″, raise ROOM_H and CAR_H and re-run the script.

Two phases

Phase 1 · woodworking: the house, finished and fully playable. A weekend of cutting plus a few evenings of finish.

Phase 2 · printing: the elevator car, crank winch, furniture and nameplate. The elevator's wood (two shaft panels, guide grooves) is cut in Phase 1 so nothing has to be retrofitted.

03Tools

Cutting & shaping

  • Table saw with a sharp plywood blade (dadoes, bevels, final sizing). A circular saw with a straightedge or track to break down the sheet first.
  • Dado stack, or repeated passes with a standard blade
  • Jigsaw with a fine plywood blade, plus a 3⅝″ hole saw if you have one (round window)
  • Router with a ⅛″ round-over bit
  • Drill/driver, #6 countersink pilot bit, ⅜″ bit for cutout corners, ¼″ bit (reaming printed bores)
  • Files and a sanding block or random-orbit sander

Layout & assembly

  • Calipers (measure the plywood. Really.)
  • Combination square, tape, pencil
  • 4+ bar clamps ≥ 24″, a few spring clamps
  • Bevel gauge (set once to 33.7°, reused for every roof cut)
  • Painter's tape for labels and masking

PPE

  • Safety glasses, hearing protection, dust mask or respirator for sanding
  • Push sticks and a riving knife or splitter on the table saw

06Assembly

Glue and screw everything. Pre-drill and countersink every screw. Plywood edges split if you skip the pilot hole. Dry-fit each step before the glue comes out.

  1. Measure the plywood and update the model

    "½″" Baltic birch is usually 12 mm (0.472″) but varies. Measure 4–5 spots with calipers, average them, set T in tools/house_model.py and re-run. Every dado width, panel length and drawing follows.

    cut list regenerated with your T before any cut

  2. Break down the sheet

    Follow the sheet layout. Rough-cut the three big strips with a circular saw, then size each part on the table saw. Put a painter's-tape label on every part with its name and an arrow pointing to the front edge.

    a full 5×5 sheet is awkward and heavy. Rough-cut it on foam insulation board on the floor, not on the table saw.

  3. Cut the middle-floor dadoes

    Inside faces of both side walls, lower edge 9″ from the bottom, width = T, depth ¼″. Lay the two walls side by side as installed and mark INSIDE on each before cutting, so you get a mirror pair. Test the fit on an offcut first: it should go in by hand, no mallet.

    offcut slides in snug; both dadoes at the same height

  4. Cut the elevator guide grooves now

    ¼″ wide × 3/16″ deep, full length, centred 1⅝″ from the back edge. One goes on the outside of the right wall, one on the inside of the shaft outer panel. Two minutes on the table saw today; nearly impossible once the house is glued up.

  5. Bevels: roof panels and eave cleats

    Set the blade to 33.7° once and do every angled cut. Roof panels: bevel both 11″ ends so the bevels are parallel (the panel's cross-section is a parallelogram). Cleats: stand a 24″+ length of 1×2 on edge, wide face against the fence, blade tilted away from the fence, and rip so each cleat is 1″ wide × ⅔″ tall. Then crosscut two pieces to length.

    never rip a short or narrow piece. Bevel the long board, then crosscut. Use a push stick and keep the guard or splitter on.

  6. Openings

    Facade (door, three windows, round attic window), left-wall windows, divider doorways and the rear-gable triangle. Drill a ⅜″ hole at each inside corner first. That gives a built-in radius and a jigsaw starting point. File or sand the edges smooth.

    run a fingertip around every opening: no splinters, no sharp corners

  7. Round over and pre-sand

    ⅛″ round-over on every edge a kid will touch: all opening edges (both faces), the open-back edges of the walls, floors and dividers, the base perimeter, and the roof's front and eave edges. Don't round surfaces that get glued or edges that butt into the facade. Sand everything to 180.

    rout edges on the larger panels; keep hands well clear of the bit on small parts, or use a router table with a featherboard.

  8. Pre-paint the interiors (recommended)

    Prime and paint the room surfaces (inside wall faces, floor tops, divider faces, ceiling undersides) before glue-up. Mask every glue zone with painter's tape. Glue doesn't bond to paint. Painting inside a finished 9″ room is miserable.

  9. Carcass: side walls + middle floor + ground divider

    Glue the middle floor into both dadoes and clamp across the walls. Measure both diagonals; they must match. Set the ground divider centred under the middle floor (on the 12″ centre line) and fix it with 2 screws down through the middle floor.

    diagonals within 1/16″; floor flush with the walls' back edges

  10. Upper divider + attic floor

    Glue the upper divider on the middle floor, directly above the ground divider. Glue the side-wall tops and the divider top, lay the attic floor on, and screw down: 3 screws into each wall, 2 into the divider. The upper divider's bottom edge is glue-only, held by the facade screws later.

  11. Base

    Mark the footprint on the base: carcass ¾″ in from the left edge, 1½″ back from the front edge (the doorstep), 2″ porch behind. Glue and screw up from underneath: 3 screws into each wall, 2 into the ground divider.

  12. Facade

    Pencil the hidden floor and divider lines on the facade (see the drawing). Glue all the front edges and screw through: 4 into each wall, 3 into the middle floor, 3 into the attic floor, 2 into each divider.

    facade sides flush with the walls; door sits on the base

  13. Rear gable frame and eave cleats

    Stand the frame on the attic floor, flush with the back. Glue it and drive 3 screws up through the attic floor from the upper rooms. Glue the cleats along both outer edges of the attic floor, bevel facing up and out; pin or clamp them.

  14. Roof

    Dry-fit both panels: they should meet in the ridge joint, sit on the cleats, the facade gable and the frame, and overhang the front by 1″. Glue all contact edges and the ridge, then drive 3 screws per panel into the cleats about ½″ from the eave. Tape across the ridge to clamp it.

    no rock when you press on the ridge; eaves flush with the side walls

  15. Fill and final sand

    Fill screw heads and any edge voids, sand 180 → 220, and do one more fingertip pass over every edge.

07Finishing

  1. Prime, paint, clear-coat

    Water-based primer, then 2 coats of colour, then 2–3 coats of water-based satin polyurethane. Scuff with 320 between clear coats. If your spray booth is up and running, the primer and clear coat lay down much nicer sprayed than brushed.

    use water-based products only. US interior paint is lead-free by law; skip oil-based and solvent-heavy finishes on a toy.

  2. Cure a full week

    "Dry to touch" isn't cured. Give it 7 days in a warm, ventilated room before it becomes a toy or gets decals.

  3. Feet, anchor, theme

    Screw on 6 rubber feet (4 corners + 2 under the elevator side). Install the anti-tip strap with the facade toward the wall. Add the licensed decals inside the rooms and move the figures in.

Small-parts test: a part can't pass the 1¼″ (31.75 mm) test cylinder if its second-smallest dimension is bigger than that. The column shows that dimension. Bed check assumes the 220 × 220 × 250 mm volume of the Ender-3 V3 SE / KE / V3; the V3 Plus is bigger.

Starting print settings

  • PLA, 0.4 mm nozzle, 0.2 mm layers
  • 3–4 walls (perimeters), 15–25% gyroid infill
  • No supports unless noted; brim on the tall housing if corners lift
  • Watch the first layer of every new part, and don't leave early prints running unattended

Post-processing

  • Knock off stringing, sand layer-line edges you can feel with a fingertip
  • Ream axle bores with a ¼″ drill bit by hand
  • Check every part again for sharp tips at the seam

09Elevator build phase 2

A car rides in a plywood shaft on the house's right side, guided by tabs in the two grooves you cut in step 4. A crank winch on top lifts it on an enclosed cord. About 3 turns take it from the ground floor to the upper floor. An O-ring adds just enough friction that it stays wherever you let go. The car only ever hangs on the cord, so lowering it can't push down on fingers.

  1. Shaft panels

    Glue and screw the shaft front panel to the right wall's outside face (3 screws from inside the right-hand rooms into its edge). Glue the outer panel against the front panel's back face and screw through the front panel into it. Screw both up from under the base.

    the two guide grooves face each other and are parallel: check with the car before the glue sets

  2. Stop block

    Glue a ½ × ¾″ plywood scrap into the front-outer corner of the shaft with its underside 13½″ above the base. The car roof hits it when the car floor lines up with the upper floor.

  3. Fit the car

    Wax the grooves and drop the car in from the top. It should fall freely under its own weight. If it binds, sand the tabs, not the grooves.

  4. Winch

    Ream the drum and crank bores to ¼″. Thread the cord through the drum's anchor hole and knot it. Set the drum in the housing, slide the axle in from the crank side through both bearing bosses and the drum, then glue the drum to the axle. Slip the O-ring on outside the housing and glue the crank on, squeezing the O-ring about 20%.

    the drum turns smoothly by hand and holds a 60 g load anywhere

  5. Rig and mount

    Tie the cord to the car's eye so it's just taut with the car on the base. Screw the lid on, then the housing onto the shaft panel edges (3 × #6 × ¾″, from inside before the lid goes on).

    crank up to the stop, crank down to the base: no binding, no slack loops

10Safety

Tip-over is the big one

The model puts this at ≈27 lb with a fairly high centre of gravity. A horizontal pull of only 7.2 lb at the ridge tips it toward a kid playing at the open back, and 5.7 lb tips it the other way (13.8 lb sideways). A 4-year-old pulling up on the roof can easily do that. Strap it to a wall stud with a furniture anti-tip kit, facade toward the wall. That's the same thing commercial dollhouses ship with. The 2″ back porch already adds about 2 lb of margin.

Pre-handoff checklist

  • Anchored to a wall stud; base sits flat, no rocking
  • No part (or broken-off piece) passes a toilet-paper tube, about 1¼″
  • No magnets, no button batteries, no glass anywhere
  • Every screw head countersunk and filled; nothing loose inside
  • Every edge and opening rounded; fingertip test passes
  • No gaps between 3/16″ and ⅜″ where a finger can get stuck (car-to-shaft gap is ⅛″)
  • No hinges or lids that can slam: the roof is fixed on purpose
  • Elevator cord fully inside the shaft, both ends tied, no loops; about 14″ total
  • Crank knob is fixed to the arm (no spinning sleeve to pinch skin)
  • Axle ends hidden: one inside the housing wall, one inside the crank hub
  • Water-based finishes only, and fully cured (7 days)
  • Feet are screwed on, not stick-on

In the shop

  • Table saw: riving knife or splitter on, push sticks for anything under 6″ wide, and never rip short or narrow stock: bevel long, then crosscut. A featherboard holds the on-edge cleat rip against the fence.
  • Router: secure the workpiece; rout edges on large panels; small parts go on a router table with hold-downs.
  • Dust: Baltic birch sanding dust is fine and gets everywhere. Wear a mask or respirator and run a shop vac on the sander.
  • Printer: keep it ventilated, keep little hands away from the hot end and bed, and stay nearby for the first few prints.
  • Glue-up with a kid "helping": hand him the painting and decals, not the drill.

Ongoing

Every month or so: check for screws backing out, new splinters where the finish has worn, cracked printed parts, and a fraying cord.
